## Summary

The system processes complex queries by integrating plan caching, a sequence of five specific optimizer rules, and detailed timing for both optimization and execution. When a query is received, the `QueryPlanner` first checks its cache for a pre-existing, optimized execution plan. A cache hit allows for immediate execution, significantly reducing latency.

In the event of a cache miss, the `QueryPlanner` generates an initial, unoptimized plan. This plan is then handed to the `QueryOptimizer`, which applies five distinct rules: Predicate Pushdown, Projection Pushdown, Constant Folding, Index Selection, and Join Reordering. These rules systematically transform the plan to enhance its efficiency and reduce its estimated cost. The `QueryOptimizer` meticulously measures the time taken for this entire optimization phase.

Once optimized, the resulting plan is stored in the `QueryPlanner`'s cache for future reuse, preventing redundant optimization for identical queries. Finally, the `ExecutionEngine` takes this optimized plan and executes its steps, while also tracking the total time required for the actual data processing. This comprehensive approach ensures that complex queries are processed efficiently, with performance gains from caching and rule-based optimization, all while providing clear metrics on the time spent in both planning and execution stages.
